Skywake

Name chosen for now. Earlier alternates: Flotsam, Caravan.

A rickety train of flying ships, crewed by animals, hunting sea monsters and fishing their cargo out of the water.

GenreRoguelike auto-runner
PlatformMobile web, portrait
InputOne thumb, tap only
LookCute animals, simple, readable

Where the fun is

While sailing: where does the thumb go

Every tap spent grabbing loot is a tap not spent shooting, and the other way round. The wave design has to keep that choice alive, not let one side win by default.

At the dock: upgrade wisely

Money is short and slots are fewer. Picking the car and the cell that keeps the run going, instead of the one that looks strongest, is the build game.

If the first prototype proves these two, the rest is content. If it does not, nothing else matters yet.

The ship

The ship is a chain of up to nine cars on a 3×3 grid. Each car is one module plus the animal who lives in it. The ship flies left, always. Where a car sits decides what it can face, so placement is the build decision.

Corner cells face two directions and are the premium spots. The center cell faces nothing and always holds the engine or command car. If that car is destroyed, the run ends. Everything else is replaceable.

Cars

Each car has its own HP and repairs at docks. A destroyed car falls into the water lane. The car behind it is now exposed and takes hits until the next dock. The chain never breaks. The center car never repairs: its HP is the run's true health bar.

Animals

An animal is welded to its car: one passive, one preferred facing, a few reaction lines. The module is the mechanic, the animal is the reason you care. Cars leveling up and gaining skills is planned, but not for the first prototype.

One verb

Tap loot to grab it by hand. Tap an enemy to fire every manual weapon at it. Collectors and auto-turrets work on their own. Nothing is dragged, aimed, or held.

Early on the thumb is split between loot and enemies. A good net on the water row is the upgrade that gives the thumb back. That is the power curve, and it needs no tutorial.

The loop

1

Enemies arrive

From the front, sometimes above. Small fry and huge ones.

2

Kill drops cargo

Everything an enemy carried falls into the water.

3

Cargo drifts under you

Same lane as natural flotsam and your own fallen cars.

4

Catch it or lose it

Nets grab it. A tap grabs it. Miss it and it is gone.

5

Dock and build

Spend it on cars, rearrange the grid, recruit animals.

A fallen car uses the same lane. Catch it and the animal comes back damaged. Miss it and the animal is gone for the run. Combat feeds the water lane twice, so watching the water is never optional.

Run structure

  • Legs. Continuous scrolling between docks. No branching map. All choices happen at the dock, because sailing is already busy.
  • Docks. Shop, rearrange, recruit, repair everything except the center car, and one event card.
  • Bosses. Every third dock. A boss fills the whole left side of the screen.
  • Biomes. Calm sea has more flotsam, fewer enemies. Storm sea reverses it. Ice sea slows loot so nets matter more.
  • Progression. In-run through cars and placement. Meta through unlocked animals and cars.

Balancing

Enemy waves and difficulty curve are the hard part, so they live in data, not code. The prototype loads plain CSV files at start. Tweak a value, reload, play. Six tables:

FileOne row isColumns to start with
settings.csva global knobstarting scrap, loot cadence, tap radius, rescue HP share, dock offers, calm before docking
legs.csva legname, dock forecast, enemy hp and damage multipliers, loot multiplier, boss
enemies.csvan enemy typeid, lane, behavior (hold, thief, charger), hp, armor, speed, damage, fire_rate, stay_s, target_rows or target_cols, charge_dmg, charge_break, cargo_count, cargo_value, size
waves.csvone spawn event in a legleg, time_s, enemy_id, count, spacing_s, note
cars.csva car tierid, animal, tier, upgrade_to, allowed_facing, hp, cost, damage, fire_rate, auto, collect_radius, reel_s, lifts, passive
loot.csva flotsam typeid, value, drift_speed, spawn_weight, net_ok

Legs are tuned by editing waves.csv only. Rewards live on the enemy, so making a wave harder and making it pay better are separate dials.

Decisions so far

QuestionCall
Losing a car setChain never breaks. The car behind becomes exposed. Drama comes from the rescue, not the collapse.
Center car setAlways the engine or command car. Destroyed means the run is over. It is the one car you protect.
Route choices setOnly at docks. Linear legs.
Loot pickup setBoth by hand and by collectors. Collectors are the progression.
Animals setDistinct but cheap: passive, facing, voice. Six for the prototype.
Car leveling and skills setYes, in a later prototype. First prototype ships cars as fixed stats so the loop can be judged on its own.
Damage model setPer-car HP, repair only at docks. The center car never repairs, so every hit it takes is permanent for the run. Armor cars in front earn their slot.
Name and look setSkywake. Cute animals, simple shapes, easy to read on a phone. Rickety machinery, real sea monsters.
Enemies from behind setNone for now. The rear column is utility: storage, workshop, lookout. Chaser levels can come later as a biome twist.
Theme undertone setYes, later. Animals versus the Leviathan, in the Hobbes sense of the state, as subtext only: the Leviathan is the final boss, docks carry posters, nobody gives a speech. Not in the first prototype.
Balancing setAll enemy, wave, car and loot numbers live in CSV files the game reads at load. See Balancing.
Aimed enemies setPass 1, after the first phone test ("no strategy"). Each enemy type may only hit certain rows or columns: skiffs hug the water, gunboats the middle and bottom, gulls the front columns. Where you put armor now matters.
Enemy armor setFlat damage taken off every hit. Turrets chip, harpoons crack. The Kraken shrugs off a tier-1 cannon, which is what the dock is for.
Thieves and chargers setFrigatebirds dive for floating loot and fly off with it; kill them and they drop it. Rams wind up, then hit the front car of a row; enough damage during the wind-up interrupts them.
Car tiers setCars upgrade at the dock into their next tier row for that row's cost. Tiers are dock purchases, not XP. Cars can also be sold for half and offers re-rolled for scrap.
Nets are not free setA net is busy for a reel time after each catch, so bursts overflow it, and a tier-1 net cannot lift crates. The thumb stays in the game after the first net.
Six legs, forecast at the dock setLegs live in legs.csv with a name, multipliers and a one-line forecast shown before buying. Bosses at 3 (Kraken) and 6 (Leviathan). A leg summary card shows kills, loot caught, missed and stolen, scrap and engine HP lost.
Feel, pass 2 setSynthesised sounds (no files) with a mute toggle, haptics on Android, aim lines before every enemy shot, hit sparks, knockback, tap ripples and reticle, slow motion on overboard and boss kills, a pier that scrolls in as the train stops, boss banner with the sea going dark, animal speech bubbles, home-screen install and pause when the app is backgrounded.
Enemies leave setFound in testing. An enemy you cannot or do not kill leaves after its stay time and takes its cargo with it, so a leg always ends. Bosses never leave.

Prototype scope

  • One biome, one boss, three legs.
  • Six cars: engine, cannon, auto-turret, net, hook, armor. Six animals to match.
  • The full drop-and-catch loop, including fallen-car rescue.
  • One dock screen: buy, place, rearrange.
  • Numbers driven by the four CSV files, so waves can be tuned without touching code.
  • Cut from the first prototype: altitude control, multiple weapons per car, events, meta progression.
  • Second prototype, if the loop holds: car leveling, car skills, chaser levels.
Passes 1 (strategy) and 2 (feel) are in, live at manuacruz.github.io/skywake. Next: phone test, then pass 3 (optimization and a headless balance harness).
